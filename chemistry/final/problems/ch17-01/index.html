pH of a lactate buffer

젖산 완충 용액의 pH

Calculate the pH of a buffer that is 0.12 M0.12\,\mathrm{M}0.12M in lactic acid, CH3CH(OH)COOH\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)COOH}CH3​CH(OH)COOH, and 0.10 M0.10\,\mathrm{M}0.10M in sodium lactate. For lactic acid, Ka=1.4×10−4K_a=1.4\times10^{-4}Ka​=1.4×10−4.
Hints
  1. Use the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation.
  2. The lactate ion is the conjugate base.
Worked solution
  1. Start by identifying the conjugate pair. Lactic acid is HA\mathrm{HA}HA and lactate is A−\mathrm{A^-}A−, so this mixture is a buffer.
  2. Calculate pKapK_apKa​. pKa=−log⁡(1.4×10−4)=3.85pK_a=-\log(1.4\times10^{-4})=3.85pKa​=−log(1.4×10−4)=3.85.
  3. Apply Henderson–Hasselbalch. pH=pKa+log⁡([A−]/[HA])=3.85+log⁡(0.10/0.12)\mathrm{pH}=pK_a+\log([\mathrm{A^-}]/[\mathrm{HA}])=3.85+\log(0.10/0.12)pH=pKa​+log([A−]/[HA])=3.85+log(0.10/0.12).
  4. Evaluate the ratio term. log⁡(0.833)=−0.079\log(0.833)=-0.079log(0.833)=−0.079, giving pH=3.77\mathrm{pH}=3.77pH=3.77.
  5. Check. There is slightly more acid than base, so the pH should be slightly below pKapK_apKa​; 3.77<3.853.77<3.853.77<3.85 is consistent.
  6. Takeaway. Buffer pH depends mainly on the conjugate-base/acid ratio, not their absolute concentrations.
