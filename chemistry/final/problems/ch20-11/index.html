Rusting and cathodic protection of iron

철의 부식과 음극 보호

Iron has Ered∘(Fe2+/Fe)=−0.44 VE^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}/Fe})=-0.44\,\mathrm{V}Ered∘​(Fe2+/Fe)=−0.44V.

(a) Write the anode and cathode reactions for rusting in acidic moist air and name the oxidizing agent.

(b) Three iron bars are placed in pure water, dilute HCl, and dilute NaOH. In which does the iron corrode most? Why?

(c) Which of Ag (+0.80+0.80+0.80), Zn (−0.76-0.76−0.76), Cu (+0.34+0.34+0.34), Ni (−0.28-0.28−0.28) could provide cathodic protection to iron, with Ered∘E^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}Ered∘​ in volts?

(d) Compare zinc-coated (galvanized) and tin-coated iron when the coating is scratched. (Ered∘(Sn2+/Sn)=−0.14 VE^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}(\mathrm{Sn^{2+}/Sn})=-0.14\,\mathrm{V}Ered∘​(Sn2+/Sn)=−0.14V.)

철의 Ered∘(Fe2+/Fe)=−0.44 VE^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}/Fe})=-0.44\,\mathrm{V}Ered∘​(Fe2+/Fe)=−0.44V이다.

(a) 산성의 습한 공기에서 녹이 슬 때의 anode와 cathode 반응을 쓰고 산화제를 쓰시오.

(b) 철 막대 세 개를 순수한 물, 묽은 HCl, 묽은 NaOH에 각각 넣었다. 어느 쪽에서 철이 가장 많이 부식되는가? 이유는?

(c) Ag (+0.80+0.80+0.80), Zn (−0.76-0.76−0.76), Cu (+0.34+0.34+0.34), Ni (−0.28-0.28−0.28) (Ered∘E^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}Ered∘​, V) 중 철을 음극 보호할 수 있는 것은?

(d) 도금이 긁혔을 때 아연 도금(galvanized)과 주석 도금 철을 비교하시오. (Ered∘(Sn2+/Sn)=−0.14 VE^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}(\mathrm{Sn^{2+}/Sn})=-0.14\,\mathrm{V}Ered∘​(Sn2+/Sn)=−0.14V)

Hints
  1. A protecting metal must be oxidized more easily than Fe, i.e., have a more negative Ered∘E^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}Ered∘​.
  2. Rusting needs both O2\mathrm{O_2}O2​ and H2O\mathrm{H_2O}H2​O; acid and salts speed it up; high pH slows it.
한글 힌트
  1. 보호 금속은 Fe보다 더 쉽게 산화되어야 한다. 즉 Ered∘E^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}Ered∘​가 더 음수여야 한다.
  2. 녹슬려면 O2\mathrm{O_2}O2​와 H2O\mathrm{H_2O}H2​O가 모두 필요하다. 산과 염은 부식을 가속하고 높은 pH는 늦춘다.
Worked solution
Answer
(a) Anode Fe→Fe2++2e−\mathrm{Fe\rightarrow Fe^{2+}+2e^-}Fe→Fe2++2e−, cathode O2+4H++4e−→2H2O\mathrm{O_2+4H^++4e^-\rightarrow2H_2O}O2​+4H++4e−→2H2​O; oxidizing agent O2\mathrm{O_2}O2​; (b) dilute HCl; (c) only Zn; (d) Zn protects even when scratched, Sn does not
  1. (a) Half-reactions. Anode (tiny anodic regions on the iron): Fe(s)→Fe2+(aq)+2e−\mathrm{Fe(s)\rightarrow Fe^{2+}(aq)+2e^-}Fe(s)→Fe2+(aq)+2e−. Cathode: O2(g)+4H+(aq)+4e−→2H2O(l)\mathrm{O_2(g)+4H^+(aq)+4e^-\rightarrow2H_2O(l)}O2​(g)+4H+(aq)+4e−→2H2​O(l). Ered∘(O2)=+1.23≫−0.44E^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}(\mathrm{O_2})=+1.23\gg-0.44Ered∘​(O2​)=+1.23≫−0.44, so iron is oxidized spontaneously; the oxidizing agent is O2\mathrm{O_2}O2​.
  2. (a) Rust formation. Fe2+\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}Fe2+ is further oxidized to Fe3+\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}Fe3+ and precipitates as rust, Fe2O3⋅xH2O\mathrm{Fe_2O_3\cdot xH_2O}Fe2​O3​⋅xH2​O.
  3. (b) Most corrosion. The dilute HCl beaker: acid supplies H+\mathrm{H^+}H+ for the cathode reaction and conducts ions. In base (high pH, above about 9) O2\mathrm{O_2}O2​ reduction is less favorable and iron hardly corrodes; pure water is intermediate.
  4. (c) Choose a sacrificial metal. It must have Ered∘<−0.44 VE^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}<-0.44\,\mathrm{V}Ered∘​<−0.44V: Zn (−0.76-0.76−0.76) qualifies. Ni (−0.28-0.28−0.28), Cu (+0.34+0.34+0.34), and Ag (+0.80+0.80+0.80) are less active than Fe and would make Fe the anode.
  5. (d) Galvanized iron. Zn is more easily oxidized than Fe. Even when scratched, Zn is the anode and corrodes while Fe is the cathode where O2\mathrm{O_2}O2​ is reduced: cathodic protection continues.
  6. (d) Tin-plated iron. Sn is less active than Fe (−0.14>−0.44-0.14>-0.44−0.14>−0.44). Once the coating breaks, Fe becomes the anode and corrodes even faster: tin gives no cathodic protection (only a barrier).
  7. Takeaway. Compare Ered∘E^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}Ered∘​: the metal with the more negative value is the anode and is sacrificed; the oxidizing agent in rusting is O2\mathrm{O_2}O2​, and acid and salts accelerate it.
