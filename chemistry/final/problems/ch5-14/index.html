Bond-enthalpy estimate versus the true ΔH for HBr

HBr 생성에서 결합 엔탈피 추정값과 참값

Consider the reaction H2(g)+Br2(l)→2HBr(g)\mathrm{H_2(g)+Br_2(l)\rightarrow 2HBr(g)}H2​(g)+Br2​(l)→2HBr(g).

(a) Use the bond enthalpies D(H−H)=436D(\mathrm{H-H})=436D(H−H)=436, D(Br−Br)=193D(\mathrm{Br-Br})=193D(Br−Br)=193, and D(H−Br)=366 kJ/molD(\mathrm{H-Br})=366\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}D(H−Br)=366kJ/mol to estimate ΔH\Delta HΔH, ignoring the fact that bromine is in the liquid state.

(b) Without doing a calculation, predict whether your estimate is more negative or less negative than the true reaction enthalpy.

(c) Using ΔHf∘(HBr, g)=−36.3 kJ/mol\Delta H_f^\circ(\mathrm{HBr,\ g})=-36.3\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}ΔHf∘​(HBr, g)=−36.3kJ/mol, determine the true reaction enthalpy.

(a) 브로민이 액체라는 점을 무시하고 결합 엔탈피 D(H−H)=436D(\mathrm{H-H})=436D(H−H)=436, D(Br−Br)=193D(\mathrm{Br-Br})=193D(Br−Br)=193, D(H−Br)=366 kJ/molD(\mathrm{H-Br})=366\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}D(H−Br)=366kJ/mol로 ΔH\Delta HΔH를 추정하시오.

(b) 계산하지 말고, 추정값이 참값보다 더 음수인지 덜 음수인지 예측하시오.

(c) ΔHf∘(HBr, g)=−36.3 kJ/mol\Delta H_f^\circ(\mathrm{HBr,\ g})=-36.3\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}ΔHf∘​(HBr, g)=−36.3kJ/mol을 이용해 참 반응 엔탈피를 구하시오.

Hints
  1. ΔH≈∑D(bonds broken)−∑D(bonds formed)\Delta H\approx\sum D(\text{bonds broken})-\sum D(\text{bonds formed})ΔH≈∑D(bonds broken)−∑D(bonds formed).
  2. Bond enthalpies are tabulated for gaseous species, so using them for Br2(l)\mathrm{Br_2(l)}Br2​(l) skips the energy needed to vaporize it.
한글 힌트
  1. ΔH≈∑D(끊는 결합)−∑D(형성 결합)\Delta H\approx\sum D(\text{끊는 결합})-\sum D(\text{형성 결합})ΔH≈∑D(끊는 결합)−∑D(형성 결합).
  2. 결합 엔탈피 표는 기체 기준이므로 Br2(l)\mathrm{Br_2(l)}Br2​(l)에 그대로 쓰면 기화에 필요한 에너지가 빠진다.
Worked solution
Answer
(a) ΔH≈−103 kJ\Delta H\approx-103\,\mathrm{kJ}ΔH≈−103kJ; (b) more negative than the true value; (c) ΔH∘=−72.6 kJ\Delta H^\circ=-72.6\,\mathrm{kJ}ΔH∘=−72.6kJ
  1. Part (a): count the bonds that change. Break one H−H\mathrm{H-H}H−H and one Br−Br\mathrm{Br-Br}Br−Br bond; form two H−Br\mathrm{H-Br}H−Br bonds.
  2. Evaluate both sums. Broken: 436+193=629 kJ436+193=629\,\mathrm{kJ}436+193=629kJ. Formed: 2(366)=732 kJ2(366)=732\,\mathrm{kJ}2(366)=732kJ. Hence ΔH≈629−732=−103 kJ\Delta H\approx629-732=-103\,\mathrm{kJ}ΔH≈629−732=−103kJ.
  3. Part (b): account for the liquid. The table value for Br−Br\mathrm{Br-Br}Br−Br refers to gaseous bromine, so the estimate silently starts from Br2(g)\mathrm{Br_2(g)}Br2​(g). The real reaction must first vaporize Br2(l)\mathrm{Br_2(l)}Br2​(l), which absorbs energy, so it releases less heat overall: the estimate is more negative than the truth.
  4. Part (c): use formation enthalpies. Both H2(g)\mathrm{H_2(g)}H2​(g) and Br2(l)\mathrm{Br_2(l)}Br2​(l) are standard-state elements with ΔHf∘=0\Delta H_f^\circ=0ΔHf∘​=0, so ΔH∘=2(−36.3)−0=−72.6 kJ\Delta H^\circ=2(-36.3)-0=-72.6\,\mathrm{kJ}ΔH∘=2(−36.3)−0=−72.6kJ.
  5. Compare the two answers. −72.6 kJ-72.6\,\mathrm{kJ}−72.6kJ is indeed less negative than the −103 kJ-103\,\mathrm{kJ}−103kJ estimate, confirming the prediction in part (b); the roughly 31 kJ31\,\mathrm{kJ}31kJ gap is essentially the enthalpy of vaporization of bromine.
  6. Takeaway. Bond enthalpies are gas-phase averages — whenever a reactant or product is a liquid or solid, predict the direction of the error before trusting the number.
