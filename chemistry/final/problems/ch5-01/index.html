Heat released by methane combustion

Methane burns at constant pressure according to
CH4(g)+2O2(g)→CO2(g)+2H2O(l)\mathrm{CH_4(g)+2O_2(g)\rightarrow CO_2(g)+2H_2O(l)}CH4​(g)+2O2​(g)→CO2​(g)+2H2​O(l) ΔH=−890 kJ\Delta H=-890\,\mathrm{kJ}ΔH=−890kJ.

How much heat is transferred when 4.50 g4.50\,\mathrm{g}4.50g of CH4\mathrm{CH_4}CH4​ burns completely? State whether heat enters or leaves the reacting system. (MCH4=16.04 g/molM_{\mathrm{CH_4}}=16.04\,\mathrm{g/mol}MCH4​​=16.04g/mol)
Hints
  1. Treat the thermochemical equation as a conversion factor: −890 kJ-890\,\mathrm{kJ}−890kJ per mole of CH4\mathrm{CH_4}CH4​.
  2. Convert grams of methane to moles before using ΔH\Delta HΔH.
Worked solution
  1. Start with the balanced thermochemical equation. Its coefficient is 1 for CH4\mathrm{CH_4}CH4​, so the stated ΔH=−890 kJ\Delta H=-890\,\mathrm{kJ}ΔH=−890kJ applies to each mole of methane consumed.
  2. Convert mass to moles. nCH4=(4.50 g)/(16.04 g/mol)=0.2805 moln_{\mathrm{CH_4}}=(4.50\,\mathrm{g})/(16.04\,\mathrm{g/mol})=0.2805\,\mathrm{mol}nCH4​​=(4.50g)/(16.04g/mol)=0.2805mol.
  3. Scale the reaction enthalpy. qp=(0.2805 mol)(−890 kJ/mol)=−2.50×102 kJq_p=(0.2805\,\mathrm{mol})(-890\,\mathrm{kJ/mol})=-2.50\times10^2\,\mathrm{kJ}qp​=(0.2805mol)(−890kJ/mol)=−2.50×102kJ. At constant pressure, qp=ΔHq_p=\Delta Hqp​=ΔH for this amount.
  4. Interpret the sign. The negative value means the reacting system releases heat; 250 kJ250\,\mathrm{kJ}250kJ flows to the surroundings.
  5. Takeaway. A thermochemical ΔH\Delta HΔH scales with the reaction amount and reverses sign if the chemical equation is reversed.
