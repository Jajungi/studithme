Spontaneity temperature range for melting ice

얼음이 녹는 과정의 자발적 온도 범위

Consider the melting of ice to liquid water at a pressure of 101.3 kPa101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}101.3kPa.

(a) Is this process endothermic or exothermic?

(b) In what temperature range is it a spontaneous process?

(c) In what temperature range is it nonspontaneous?

(d) At what temperature are the two phases in equilibrium?
한글
101.3 kPa101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}101.3kPa에서 얼음이 녹아 액체 물이 되는 과정을 생각하자.

(a) 이 과정은 흡열인가 발열인가?

(b) 어느 온도 범위에서 자발적인가?

(c) 어느 온도 범위에서 비자발적인가?

(d) 어느 온도에서 두 상이 평형을 이루는가?

Hints
  1. Melting requires heat, and the liquid is more disordered than the solid: determine the signs of ΔH\Delta HΔH and ΔS\Delta SΔS.
  2. ΔG=ΔH−TΔS\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta SΔG=ΔH−TΔS; for ΔH>0\Delta H>0ΔH>0, ΔS>0\Delta S>0ΔS>0 spontaneity needs high TTT.
한글 힌트
  1. 녹는 데는 열이 필요하고 액체는 고체보다 무질서하다. ΔH\Delta HΔH와 ΔS\Delta SΔS의 부호를 정한다.
  2. ΔG=ΔH−TΔS\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta SΔG=ΔH−TΔS이며, ΔH>0\Delta H>0ΔH>0, ΔS>0\Delta S>0ΔS>0이면 높은 TTT에서 자발적이다.
Worked solution
Answer
(a) endothermic; (b) above 0 ∘C0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}0∘C; (c) below 0 ∘C0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}0∘C; (d) 0 ∘C0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}0∘C (273.15 K273.15\,\mathrm{K}273.15K)
  1. Part (a): sign of ΔH\Delta HΔH. Melting breaks hydrogen bonds in the solid and needs heat: ΔH>0\Delta H>0ΔH>0 — endothermic.
  2. Signs for the next parts. The liquid is more disordered than the solid, so ΔS>0\Delta S>0ΔS>0. With ΔH>0\Delta H>0ΔH>0 and ΔS>0\Delta S>0ΔS>0, ΔG=ΔH−TΔS\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta SΔG=ΔH−TΔS turns negative only above a crossover temperature.
  3. Part (b): spontaneous range. Spontaneous for T>0 ∘CT>0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}T>0∘C (273.15 K273.15\,\mathrm{K}273.15K) at 101.3 kPa101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}101.3kPa.
  4. Part (c): nonspontaneous range. Nonspontaneous for T<0 ∘CT<0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}T<0∘C (water freezes instead).
  5. Part (d): equilibrium. At the crossover ΔG=0\Delta G=0ΔG=0, i.e. T=ΔH/ΔS=0 ∘CT=\Delta H/\Delta S=0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}T=ΔH/ΔS=0∘C (273.15 K), the normal melting point.
  6. Takeaway. For ΔH>0\Delta H>0ΔH>0, ΔS>0\Delta S>0ΔS>0 the process is entropy-driven and favored at high temperature; the boundary T=ΔH/ΔST=\Delta H/\Delta ST=ΔH/ΔS is the phase-transition temperature.
