Nickel concentration cell

니켈 농도 전지

A concentration cell consists of two Ni electrodes in Ni2+\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}Ni2+ solutions of 1.00×10−3 M1.00\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{M}1.00×10−3M and 1.00 M1.00\,\mathrm{M}1.00M, joined by a salt bridge, at 298 K298\,\mathrm{K}298K.

(a) What is Ecell∘E^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}Ecell∘​?

(b) Which half-cell is the anode, and in which direction do electrons flow?

(c) Calculate the cell potential.

(d) What happens to [Ni2+][\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}][Ni2+] in each compartment as the cell operates, and when does it stop?

Hints
  1. The two electrodes are identical, so the standard potentials cancel.
  2. The reaction transfers Ni2+\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}Ni2+ from the concentrated side to the dilute side; Q=[dilute]/[concentrated]Q=[\mathrm{dilute}]/[\mathrm{concentrated}]Q=[dilute]/[concentrated].
한글 힌트
  1. 두 전극이 같으므로 표준 전위가 상쇄된다.
  2. 반응은 Ni2+\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}Ni2+를 진한 쪽에서 묽은 쪽으로 옮긴다. Q=[묽은 쪽]/[진한 쪽]Q=[\text{묽은 쪽}]/[\text{진한 쪽}]Q=[묽은 쪽]/[진한 쪽].
Worked solution
Answer
(a) 0 V0\,\mathrm{V}0V; (b) dilute half-cell is the anode; e−e^-e− flow dilute → concentrated; (c) E=+0.089 VE=+0.089\,\mathrm{V}E=+0.089V; (d) [Ni2+][\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}][Ni2+] increases at the anode, decreases at the cathode, until both are about 0.5 M0.5\,\mathrm{M}0.5M and E=0E=0E=0
  1. (a) Standard potential. Both half-cells are Ni2+/Ni\mathrm{Ni^{2+}/Ni}Ni2+/Ni (−0.28 V-0.28\,\mathrm{V}−0.28V), so Ecell∘=(−0.28)−(−0.28)=0 VE^\circ_{\mathrm{cell}}=(-0.28)-(-0.28)=0\,\mathrm{V}Ecell∘​=(−0.28)−(−0.28)=0V.
  2. (b) Direction. The spontaneous change moves toward equal concentrations. In the dilute compartment Ni is oxidized (Ni→Ni2++2e−\mathrm{Ni\rightarrow Ni^{2+}+2e^-}Ni→Ni2++2e−) to raise [Ni2+][\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}][Ni2+]: the dilute side is the anode; the concentrated side is the cathode. Electrons flow dilute → concentrated.
  3. Overall reaction. Ni2+(1.00 M)→Ni2+(1.00×10−3 M)\mathrm{Ni^{2+}(1.00\,M)\rightarrow Ni^{2+}(1.00\times10^{-3}\,M)}Ni2+(1.00M)→Ni2+(1.00×10−3M), n=2n=2n=2, so Q=1.00×10−31.00=1.00×10−3Q=\dfrac{1.00\times10^{-3}}{1.00}=1.00\times10^{-3}Q=1.001.00×10−3​=1.00×10−3.
  4. (c) Nernst. E=0−0.05922log⁡(1.00×10−3)=−0.0296×(−3)=+0.089 VE=0-\dfrac{0.0592}{2}\log(1.00\times10^{-3})=-0.0296\times(-3)=+0.089\,\mathrm{V}E=0−20.0592​log(1.00×10−3)=−0.0296×(−3)=+0.089V.
  5. (d) Changes during operation. [Ni2+][\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}][Ni2+] rises in the dilute (anode) compartment and falls in the concentrated (cathode) compartment.
  6. (d) End point. QQQ approaches 1 when the two concentrations approach each other (about 0.50 M0.50\,\mathrm{M}0.50M each), then E=0E=0E=0 and the cell is “dead”.
  7. Takeaway. In a concentration cell E∘=0E^\circ=0E∘=0; the dilute side is the anode, and the driving force is purely the concentration ratio through −(0.0592/n)log⁡Q-(0.0592/n)\log Q−(0.0592/n)logQ.
