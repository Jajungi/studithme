Predicting signs of entropy change

엔트로피 변화 부호 예측

Predict the sign of ΔSsys\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}ΔSsys​ for each process and identify the dominant reason:

(a) H2O(l)→H2O(g)\mathrm{H_2O(l)\rightarrow H_2O(g)}H2​O(l)→H2​O(g)
(b) Ag+(aq)+Cl−(aq)→AgCl(s)\mathrm{Ag^+(aq)+Cl^-(aq)\rightarrow AgCl(s)}Ag+(aq)+Cl−(aq)→AgCl(s)
(c) 4Fe(s)+3O2(g)→2Fe2O3(s)\mathrm{4Fe(s)+3O_2(g)\rightarrow 2Fe_2O_3(s)}4Fe(s)+3O2​(g)→2Fe2​O3​(s)
(d) N2(g)+O2(g)→2NO(g)\mathrm{N_2(g)+O_2(g)\rightarrow 2NO(g)}N2​(g)+O2​(g)→2NO(g).
Hints
  1. Phase and the change in moles of gas usually dominate qualitative entropy predictions.
  2. Gas formation increases entropy; precipitation and gas consumption decrease it.
Worked solution
  1. Start with the main entropy trends. Generally S(gas)>S(liquid)>S(solid)S(\mathrm{gas})>S(\mathrm{liquid})>S(\mathrm{solid})S(gas)>S(liquid)>S(solid), and more gas particles usually means more accessible microstates.
  2. (a) Vaporization. Liquid becomes gas, greatly increasing positional freedom and accessible microstates; ΔSsys>0\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}>0ΔSsys​>0.
  3. (b) Precipitation. Dispersed aqueous ions become an ordered crystalline solid; ΔSsys<0\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}<0ΔSsys​<0.
  4. (c) Oxide formation. Three moles of gas are consumed to form only solid product; the large loss of gaseous particles makes ΔSsys<0\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}<0ΔSsys​<0.
  5. (d) Same gas mole count. Two moles of gas form two moles of gas, so the gas-count shortcut alone predicts no strong sign. Actual standard entropies give a small positive ΔS∘\Delta S^\circΔS∘ for this reaction; molecular identity matters when gas moles tie.
  6. Takeaway. Use phase and gas-mole changes first, but use tabulated S∘S^\circS∘ values when those indicators do not decide the sign.
