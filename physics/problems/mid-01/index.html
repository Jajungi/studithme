Rank flux and E on nested Gaussian surfaces

가우시안 면 선속·장 순위

The figure shows, in cross section, two Gaussian spheres and two Gaussian cubes centered on a positively charged particle +q+q+q.

(a) Rank the net flux through the four Gaussian surfaces, greatest first.
(b) Rank the magnitudes of the electric fields on the surfaces, greatest first, and indicate whether the magnitudes are uniform or variable on each surface.

Worked solution
Answer
(a) a=b=c=d; (b) a>b>c>d; spheres uniform, cubes variable
  1. Goal. Rank electric flux through four Gaussian surfaces around +q+q+q, then rank typical field magnitudes on each surface and state whether ∣E∣|E|∣E∣ is uniform there.
  2. Gauss’s law for flux. Net flux depends only on enclosed charge: ΦE=qenc/ε0\Phi_E=q_\mathrm{enc}/\varepsilon_0ΦE​=qenc​/ε0​. Every surface in the figure encloses the same single +q+q+q, regardless of size or shape.
  3. (a) Flux ranking. Therefore ΦE=q/ε0\Phi_E=q/\varepsilon_0ΦE​=q/ε0​ for surfaces aaa, bbb, ccc, and ddd — a four-way tie with no surface ahead of another.
  4. (b) Field magnitude idea. For a point charge, ∣E∣∝1/r2|E|\propto 1/r^2∣E∣∝1/r2 on average. Smaller Gaussian surfaces sit closer to +q+q+q, so they generally correspond to larger field magnitudes than larger nested surfaces.
  5. Order of ∣E∣|E|∣E∣. Smallest sphere aaa has the largest ∣E∣|E|∣E∣; then cube bbb; then larger sphere ccc; largest cube ddd has the smallest among the four: a>b>c>da>b>c>da>b>c>d.
  6. Uniform vs variable. On centered spheres aaa and ccc, ∣E∣|E|∣E∣ is uniform over the surface by symmetry. On cubes bbb and ddd, ∣E∣|E|∣E∣ is variable (stronger near corners/closer parts, weaker at face centers).
  7. Final answer. (a) a=b=c=da=b=c=da=b=c=d; (b) a>b>c>da>b>c>da>b>c>d; spheres uniform, cubes variable.
