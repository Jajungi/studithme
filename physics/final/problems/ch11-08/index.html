Copper ring in a ramping field: emf, current, and induced E

증가하는 자기장 속 구리 고리: 기전력·전류·유도 E

A copper ring of radius r=4.0 cmr=4.0\,\mathrm{cm}r=4.0cm and resistance 0.020 Ω0.020\,\Omega0.020Ω lies in a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to its plane, pointing into the page, that increases steadily at dB/dt=0.50 T/sdB/dt=0.50\,\mathrm{T/s}dB/dt=0.50T/s. (a) Find the induced emf and its direction. (b) Find the current and the power dissipated. (c) Find the magnitude of the induced electric field at the ring. (d) Suppose the ring is removed. What is the electric field at the same location? (e) If the radius of the circle is doubled (field still uniform over it), how do ε\varepsilonε and EEE change?

(b) 전류와 소비 전력은? (c) 고리 위치의 유도 전기장 크기는? (d) 고리를 치우면 같은 위치의 전기장은? (e) 원의 반지름을 두 배로 하면(자기장이 그 원 안에서 균일) ε\varepsilonε과 EEE는 어떻게 변하는가?

Hints
  1. ε=πr2 dB/dt\varepsilon=\pi r^{2}\,dB/dtε=πr2dB/dt for a uniform field changing in time.
  2. The tangential field is E=ε/(2πr)E=\varepsilon/(2\pi r)E=ε/(2πr).
  3. The field exists whether or not a conductor is present.
한글 힌트
  1. 균일한 장이 시간에 따라 변하면 ε=πr2 dB/dt\varepsilon=\pi r^{2}\,dB/dtε=πr2dB/dt.
  2. 접선 전기장 E=ε/(2πr)E=\varepsilon/(2\pi r)E=ε/(2πr).
  3. 전기장은 도체가 있든 없든 존재한다.
Worked solution
Answer
(a) 2.5 mV2.5\,\mathrm{mV}2.5mV CCW; (b) 0.13 A0.13\,\mathrm{A}0.13A, 3.2×10−4 W3.2\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{W}3.2×10−4W; (c) E=0.010 V/mE=0.010\,\mathrm{V/m}E=0.010V/m; (d) unchanged; (e) ε×4\varepsilon\times4ε×4, E×2E\times2E×2
  1. Concept. Faraday: ε=−dΦB/dt\varepsilon=-d\Phi_B/dtε=−dΦB​/dt. A changing B⃗\vec BB induces a tangential E⃗\vec EE with ∮E⃗⋅dl⃗=−dΦB/dt\oint\vec E\cdot d\vec l=-d\Phi_B/dt∮E⋅dl=−dΦB​/dt; the ring merely reveals it by letting charge flow.
  2. (a) Emf. ∣ε∣=πr2 dB/dt=π(0.040)2(0.50)=2.5×10−3 V|\varepsilon|=\pi r^{2}\,dB/dt=\pi(0.040)^{2}(0.50)=2.5\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V}∣ε∣=πr2dB/dt=π(0.040)2(0.50)=2.5×10−3V. Flux into the page increases, so the induced field must point out of the page: counterclockwise current (Lenz).
  3. (b) Current and power. I=ε/R=2.51×10−3/0.020=0.13 AI=\varepsilon/R=2.51\times10^{-3}/0.020=0.13\,\mathrm{A}I=ε/R=2.51×10−3/0.020=0.13A and P=I2R=(0.1257)2(0.020)=3.2×10−4 WP=I^{2}R=(0.1257)^{2}(0.020)=3.2\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{W}P=I2R=(0.1257)2(0.020)=3.2×10−4W.
  4. (c) Induced E. 2πrE=ε2\pi rE=\varepsilon2πrE=ε gives E=ε/(2πr)=2.51×10−3/(2π⋅0.040)=1.0×10−2 V/mE=\varepsilon/(2\pi r)=2.51\times10^{-3}/(2\pi\cdot0.040)=1.0\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V/m}E=ε/(2πr)=2.51×10−3/(2π⋅0.040)=1.0×10−2V/m. Equivalently E=r2 dB/dt=12(0.040)(0.50)=0.010 V/mE=\tfrac r2\,dB/dt=\tfrac12(0.040)(0.50)=0.010\,\mathrm{V/m}E=2r​dB/dt=21​(0.040)(0.50)=0.010V/m ✓.
  5. (d) Ring removed. The field is unchanged, E=0.010 V/mE=0.010\,\mathrm{V/m}E=0.010V/m, tangential and counterclockwise, even in vacuum. Only the current disappears.
  6. (e) Double radius. ε∝r2\varepsilon\propto r^{2}ε∝r2 becomes 4×4\times4× (1.0×10−2 V1.0\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V}1.0×10−2V) and E=r2 dB/dtE=\tfrac r2\,dB/dtE=2r​dB/dt becomes 2×2\times2× (0.020 V/m0.020\,\mathrm{V/m}0.020V/m).
  7. Pitfall check. ∮E⃗⋅dl⃗≠0\oint\vec E\cdot d\vec l\neq0∮E⋅dl=0, so this E⃗\vec EE is nonconservative: there is no potential difference "around the ring" that you can assign by V=−∫E⃗⋅dl⃗V=-\int\vec E\cdot d\vec lV=−∫E⋅dl.
  8. Final answer. (a) 2.5 mV2.5\,\mathrm{mV}2.5mV, CCW; (b) 0.13 A0.13\,\mathrm{A}0.13A, 3.2×10−4 W3.2\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{W}3.2×10−4W; (c) 0.010 V/m0.010\,\mathrm{V/m}0.010V/m; (d) same; (e) ε×4\varepsilon\times4ε×4, E×2E\times2E×2.
