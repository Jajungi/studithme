Discharge to 75% of the initial charge

초기 전하의 75%까지 방전

A charged capacitor is connected to a resistor and a switch as shown. The circuit has a time constant of 1.50 s1.50\,\mathrm{s}1.50s. Soon after the switch is closed, the charge on the capacitor is 75.0%75.0\%75.0% of its initial charge QQQ.

(a) Find the time interval required for the capacitor to reach this charge. (b) If R=250 kΩR=250\,\mathrm{k}\OmegaR=250kΩ, what is the value of CCC?
한글
충전된 축전기가 그림처럼 저항과 스위치에 연결되어 있다. 이 회로의 시정수는 1.50 s1.50\,\mathrm{s}1.50s이다. 스위치를 닫은 뒤 얼마 지나지 않아 축전기의 전하는 초기 전하 QQQ의 75.0%75.0\%75.0%가 되었다.

(a) 이 전하에 이르기까지 걸린 시간을 구하라. (b) R=250 kΩR=250\,\mathrm{k}\OmegaR=250kΩ이면 CCC는 얼마인가?

Hints
  1. Closing the switch puts the charged capacitor across RRR with no emf in the loop, so this is a discharge: q(t)=Qe−t/τq(t)=Qe^{-t/\tau}q(t)=Qe−t/τ.
  2. Isolate the exponential and take a natural logarithm: t=τln⁡(Q/q)t=\tau\ln(Q/q)t=τln(Q/q).
  3. For (b) use τ=RC\tau=RCτ=RC with RRR in ohms (250 kΩ=2.50×105 Ω250\,\mathrm{k}\Omega=2.50\times10^5\,\Omega250kΩ=2.50×105Ω).
한글 힌트
  1. 스위치를 닫으면 충전된 축전기가 RRR만 보고 고리에 기전력이 없으므로 방전이다: q(t)=Qe−t/τq(t)=Qe^{-t/\tau}q(t)=Qe−t/τ.
  2. 지수를 고립시키고 자연로그를 취한다: t=τln⁡(Q/q)t=\tau\ln(Q/q)t=τln(Q/q).
  3. (b)는 τ=RC\tau=RCτ=RC에 R=2.50×105 ΩR=2.50\times10^5\,\OmegaR=2.50×105Ω을 쓴다.
Worked solution
Answer
(a) t≈0.432 st\approx0.432\,\mathrm{s}t≈0.432s; (b) C=6.00 μFC=6.00\,\mu\mathrm{F}C=6.00μF
  1. Goal. Convert a charge ratio into an elapsed time, then recover the capacitance from the given time constant.
  2. Which RC formula? With the switch closed the loop contains only CCC and RRR — no source — so the charge decays: q(t)=Qe−t/τq(t)=Qe^{-t/\tau}q(t)=Qe−t/τ with τ=1.50 s\tau=1.50\,\mathrm{s}τ=1.50s. The charging form 1−e−t/τ1-e^{-t/\tau}1−e−t/τ would describe charge rising, which is not what happens here.
  3. Set up the ratio. qQ=0.750=e−t/τ\dfrac{q}{Q}=0.750=e^{-t/\tau}Qq​=0.750=e−t/τ.
  4. Take the logarithm. −t/τ=ln⁡0.750-t/\tau=\ln0.750−t/τ=ln0.750, so t=τln⁡10.750=τln⁡43t=\tau\ln\dfrac{1}{0.750}=\tau\ln\dfrac43t=τln0.7501​=τln34​.
  5. Evaluate. ln⁡(4/3)=0.2877\ln(4/3)=0.2877ln(4/3)=0.2877, so t=(1.50)(0.2877)=0.4315 s≈0.432 st=(1.50)(0.2877)=0.4315\,\mathrm{s}\approx0.432\,\mathrm{s}t=(1.50)(0.2877)=0.4315s≈0.432s.
  6. Sanity check the size. Losing only 25%25\%25% should take less than one time constant, and indeed 0.432 s<1.50 s0.432\,\mathrm{s}<1.50\,\mathrm{s}0.432s<1.50s. For comparison, half the charge would take τln⁡2=1.04 s\tau\ln2=1.04\,\mathrm{s}τln2=1.04s.
  7. Part (b). τ=RC⇒C=τR=1.50 s2.50×105 Ω=6.00×10−6 F=6.00 μF\tau=RC\Rightarrow C=\dfrac{\tau}{R}=\dfrac{1.50\,\mathrm{s}}{2.50\times10^5\,\Omega}=6.00\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{F}=6.00\,\mu\mathrm{F}τ=RC⇒C=Rτ​=2.50×105Ω1.50s​=6.00×10−6F=6.00μF.
  8. Pitfall check. ln⁡0.750\ln0.750ln0.750 is negative; flip the ratio to ln⁡(Q/q)\ln(Q/q)ln(Q/q) so the time comes out positive. And keep kΩ→Ω\mathrm{k}\Omega\to\OmegakΩ→Ω straight: forgetting the 10310^3103 changes CCC by three orders of magnitude.
  9. Final answer. (a) t≈0.432 st\approx0.432\,\mathrm{s}t≈0.432s. (b) C=6.00 μFC=6.00\,\mu\mathrm{F}C=6.00μF.
