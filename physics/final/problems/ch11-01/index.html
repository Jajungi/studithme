Motional emf of a rod moving through a magnetic field

자기장 속을 움직이는 막대의 운동 기전력

A straight metal rod of length l=0.50 ml=0.50\,\mathrm{m}l=0.50m lies along the yyy-axis and moves with constant velocity v⃗=(4.0 m/s) x^\vec v=(4.0\,\mathrm{m/s})\,\hat xv=(4.0m/s)x^ through a uniform magnetic field B⃗=0.25 T\vec B=0.25\,\mathrm{T}B=0.25T pointing into the page (−z^-\hat z−z^). (a) Which end of the rod becomes positive? (b) What electric field is established inside the rod, and what is the emf between its ends? (c) What is the emf if the same rod is moved at the same speed along its own length (along ±y^\pm\hat y±y^​)?
한글
길이 l=0.50 ml=0.50\,\mathrm{m}l=0.50m인 곧은 금속 막대가 yyy축을 따라 놓여 있고, 지면 속으로 향하는(−z^-\hat z−z^) 균일 자기장 B=0.25 TB=0.25\,\mathrm{T}B=0.25T 속에서 일정한 속도 v⃗=(4.0 m/s) x^\vec v=(4.0\,\mathrm{m/s})\,\hat xv=(4.0m/s)x^로 움직인다. (a) 어느 쪽 끝이 (+)가 되는가? (b) 막대 속에 형성되는 전기장과 양 끝 사이 기전력은? (c) 같은 막대를 같은 속력으로 막대 길이 방향(±y^\pm\hat y±y^​)으로 움직이면 기전력은?

Hints
  1. Free electrons feel F⃗=qv⃗×B⃗\vec F=q\vec v\times\vec BF=qv×B along the rod until electric repulsion balances it.
  2. Balance condition: eE=evBeE=evBeE=evB, so E=vBE=vBE=vB and ε=El\varepsilon=Elε=El.
  3. Treat a positive charge first to find the polarity: x^×(−z^)=+y^\hat x\times(-\hat z)=+\hat yx^×(−z^)=+y^​.
한글 힌트
  1. 자유 전자는 F⃗=qv⃗×B⃗\vec F=q\vec v\times\vec BF=qv×B를 받아 막대를 따라 쏠리다가 전기 반발력과 평형을 이룬다.
  2. 평형: eE=evBeE=evBeE=evB → E=vBE=vBE=vB, ε=El\varepsilon=Elε=El.
  3. 극성은 먼저 양전하로: x^×(−z^)=+y^\hat x\times(-\hat z)=+\hat yx^×(−z^)=+y^​.
Worked solution
Answer
(a) top (+y+y+y) end positive; (b) E=vB=1.0 V/mE=vB=1.0\,\mathrm{V/m}E=vB=1.0V/m, ε=vBl=0.50 V\varepsilon=vBl=0.50\,\mathrm{V}ε=vBl=0.50V; (c) 000
  1. Concept. A rod moving through B⃗\vec BB is a source of emf: ε=vBl\varepsilon=vBlε=vBl, where lll is the length perpendicular to both v⃗\vec vv and B⃗\vec BB.
  2. (a) Polarity. For a positive charge, v⃗×B⃗=(4.0)(0.25) x^×(−z^)=+1.0 y^\vec v\times\vec B=(4.0)(0.25)\,\hat x\times(-\hat z)=+1.0\,\hat yv×B=(4.0)(0.25)x^×(−z^)=+1.0y^​ (in N/C\mathrm{N/C}N/C). Positive charge is pushed toward the top (+y+y+y) end, so the top end is the +++ terminal; the electrons pile up at the bottom.
  3. (b) Field inside the rod. Charge accumulation stops when eE=evBeE=evBeE=evB, so E=vB=(4.0)(0.25)=1.0 V/mE=vB=(4.0)(0.25)=1.0\,\mathrm{V/m}E=vB=(4.0)(0.25)=1.0V/m, directed along +y^+\hat y+y^​.
  4. Emf. ε=El=vBl=(4.0)(0.25)(0.50)=0.50 V\varepsilon=El=vBl=(4.0)(0.25)(0.50)=0.50\,\mathrm{V}ε=El=vBl=(4.0)(0.25)(0.50)=0.50V. No closed circuit is needed for this potential difference to exist.
  5. (c) Motion along the rod. Now v⃗=±v y^\vec v=\pm v\,\hat yv=±vy^​, and v⃗×B⃗=∓vB x^\vec v\times\vec B=\mp vB\,\hat xv×B=∓vBx^ is perpendicular to the rod. It pushes charge sideways against the wall, not along the length, so there is no charge separation along the rod and ε=0\varepsilon=0ε=0. The rod sweeps no flux.
  6. Pitfall check. Do not forget the factor lll (ε=vB\varepsilon=vBε=vB is a field, not a voltage), and do not call the electron-accumulation end positive.
  7. Final answer. (a) top end is +++; (b) E=1.0 V/mE=1.0\,\mathrm{V/m}E=1.0V/m, ε=0.50 V\varepsilon=0.50\,\mathrm{V}ε=0.50V; (c) ε=0\varepsilon=0ε=0.
